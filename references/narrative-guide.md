# Narrative and career-output guide

## Project retrospective

Write in this order:

1. **What changed:** the final delivered result.
2. **How it unfolded:** the phases and turning points.
3. **Where it fought back:** repeated loops, reversals, and time sinks.
4. **What improved:** decisions, validation, or simplifications that broke the loop.
5. **What to do differently next time:** concrete process changes.

Avoid a chronological dump of every commit. Select events that explain a change in direction, risk, understanding, or delivery state.

## Resume bullets

Use this structure:

> [Action] [what was built or changed] across [verified scope], using [verified method], resulting in [user-confirmed outcome].

Examples with honest placeholders:

- Built a cross-platform notification workflow across four AI coding CLIs, adding local and webhook delivery paths; resulting in **[insert verified adoption or time-saving outcome]**.
- Stabilized the release pipeline by adding automated validation and reducing repeated changes in **[component]**; resulting in **[insert verified reliability outcome]**.

Never turn commit volume into an impact metric. `120 commits` may describe scope, but it does not prove value.

## STAR interview story

- **Situation:** constraints and initial state.
- **Task:** the user's responsibility, not the team's generic goal.
- **Action:** the decisive trade-offs, experiments, reversals, and validation.
- **Result:** shipped behavior and user-confirmed impact.

The most interesting evidence is often the loop that was broken, not the clean final implementation.

## Portfolio case study

Prefer one clear narrative over a wall of metrics:

1. problem;
2. constraints;
3. turning point;
4. final system;
5. evidence;
6. reflection.

Use report metrics as supporting captions, not as the story itself.
